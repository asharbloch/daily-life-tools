Marks to Percentage: A Quick Guide for Students

Turning exam marks into a percentage is a skill every student needs, whether you are checking a test result, working out a grade, or comparing scores from different papers. The good news is that the calculation is simple, and once you know the formula you can use it for any subject.

The formula

Divide the marks you obtained by the total marks available, then multiply by 100. In symbols: Percentage = (Marks obtained / Total marks) x 100. For example, if you scored 72 out of 90, divide 72 by 90 to get 0.8, then multiply by 100 to get 80%.

Worked examples

Suppose you scored 45 out of 50 in mathematics. That is 45 divided by 50, which is 0.9, or 90%. If you scored 135 out of 150 in science, the calculation is 135 divided by 150, which is also 0.9, or 90%. The two scores look different as raw marks, but as percentages they are exactly the same, which is why percentages are so useful for fair comparisons.

For a larger paper, the method does not change. If you scored 342 out of 400, divide 342 by 400 to get 0.855, which is 85.5%. If your answer has many decimal places, round it sensibly: two decimal places are usually enough for school work, so 85.55% is a clear, honest result.

Combining subjects

To find an overall percentage across several subjects, add up all the marks you obtained and divide by the total marks available across all the papers, then multiply by 100. Do not simply average the individual percentages unless every paper had the same total marks, because a paper worth 100 marks should count more than a paper worth 20.

A common mistake is to forget the final step and leave the answer as a decimal, such as 0.8 instead of 80%. Another is to divide by the wrong total, for example by the number of questions instead of the total marks. Double-check both numbers before you calculate.
